Method and sources

Back to CrimeWatch SA

How claims are labelled

Fact labels on this site follow the closed set in the MediaWatch SA methodology (section 2). Only these five labels are used on factual claims. No other fact label is invented. Each claim is labelled separately.

LabelMeaning, as set out in that methodology
ProvenDirect primary evidence checked (official text, unedited primary clip, API-verified post, court record, and the like).
EstablishedStrong secondary corroboration; reasonable people would treat as settled for practical purposes.
AllegedAsserted by a source; not independently confirmed.
DisputedCredible conflicting accounts or interpretations remain.
Not establishedInsufficient evidence; includes pure opinion when scored as if it were fact.

Official SAPS and NPA statistics record what was charged or counted, not what happened. A recorded murder, rape or kidnapping is a police or court administrative count. It is not a census of every crime that occurred, and it can be revised if cases are reclassified.

In the media-gap note on the home page, a demonstrable false statement of fact is tagged with the MediaWatch driver factual_error. That tag is a driver from the same methodology, not a sixth fact label.

House rules

  1. No invented numbers. Every figure comes from a source in the register. Where a number could not be verified, the page says so and uses Not established.
  2. Official and civil-society figures are shown side by side, with period and method stated, and are not added together or reconciled into a single “true” count.
  3. SAPS quarterly figures are police-recorded crime: 91 days in April–June 2026 (Q1 pack) and 92 days in July–September 2026 (official Q2 pack). GPSJS is a household survey for a full year and includes unreported crime. AfriForum and TLU SA keep their own farm-incident registers, on their own definitions and calendars.
  4. The official Q2 2026/27 pack, released 20 November 2026, is the source of record for the latest quarter. National headline counts on the home page were checked against the official PDF and the official Excel summary. Year-on-year changes are those published by SAPS. Quarter-on-quarter comparisons, per-day figures, and kidnapping provincial rates are arithmetic from official counts (and, for rates, official printed populations) and are described as such rather than given a separate fact label.
  5. Private victims and suspects appear only as totals. Names, home addresses, telephone numbers, identity numbers and photographs of private people are not used. Heat maps on this draft are at province level. Station lists, where shown, are official station rankings, not a map of private addresses, and go no finer than a police station.
  6. Historical studies and civil-society estimates are not multiplied by current SAPS counts or extrapolated beyond their stated period.
  7. The site uses no cookies, no analytics, no trackers and no scripts.

How the work is checked

The figures, labels and notes on this site are researched and drafted by AI research assistants working under a human owner. Where a number is derived rather than copied from a source (for example a per-day figure or a quarter-on-quarter change), it is AI-derived arithmetic from the cited official counts and is described that way.

  1. A researcher finds sources, extracts figures and drafts each page.
  2. Source reviewers check every new source that is not a major outlet or an official body before it is used.
  3. An independent critic reviews each change for errors, unsupported claims and privacy problems before it is published.

An external auditor, independent of the researcher, source reviewers and independent critic, audits the published site and repo. The audit is advisory and doesn’t block publishing. Findings are raised as public issues on this repository, and each one we act on is noted in the commit history with the commit that fixed it.
