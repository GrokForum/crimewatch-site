Murder and violent crime

Back to CrimeWatch SA

Official recorded crime for July–September 2026, unless a different period is stated. Official statistics record what was charged or counted, not everything that happened.

Murder

Proven SAPS recorded 5,590 murders in July–September 2026, against 5,794 in July–September 2025, a decline of 204 cases (−3.5%). The official ratio is 8.8 per 100,000 (2021 mid-year population series; Republic population printed in this pack: 63,743,003). Source: official Q2 PDF, slides 8 and 21.

Per-day equivalent, arithmetic from that official count: 5,590 ÷ 92 = 60.8 a day. A commonly quoted figure of about 85 murders a day is not an official current rate in the packs used here. Not established as a current official daily rate.

Proven In the SAPS 2025/26 annual-report briefing (year ended 31 March 2026), murders were 23,171, down 9.4% from 25,573, a rate of 36.72 per 100,000. That is 23,171 ÷ 365 = 63.5 a day. Previous years in the same briefing: 25,573 in 2024/25 (70.1 a day) and 27,590 in 2023/24. A ministerial reply (NW2957) instead gives 27,621 murders in 2023/24. Both official figures are shown; they are not averaged. SAPS briefing PDF · NW2957.

Compared with the previous official quarter (April–June 2026: 5,427 murders), the Q2 count is 163 higher. That quarter-on-quarter difference is arithmetic from the two official packs. SAPS itself states year-on-year changes.

Attempted murder and contact crime

Proven Attempted murder rose to 7,470 (+3.0%, from 7,255). Contact crime (crimes against the person) was 152,281 (−2.3%, from 155,936). Aggravated robbery subcategories in the same pack include carjacking 3,977 (−16.8%) and cash-in-transit robbery 36, which SAPS states as 12 counts higher than 24, without a percentage.

Causative-factor sample, including gang-related murders

Proven In the official Q2 causative-factor sample (5,396 murder counts), 261 murders were classified as gang-related, of which 208 (79.7%) were in the Western Cape. Taxi-related murders in that sample were 81, of which 47 (58.0%) were in the Western Cape. Robbery-related murders were 331, with Gauteng the highest provincial count at 93. The largest category in the sample was argument, misunderstanding, provocation or a fight (955). Source: official PDF, provincial causative-factor slide; sample size is printed on that slide.

These are sample classifications, not a complete official census of gang warfare. Not established A standalone official “gang warfare” crime series was not found in this pack.

Proven Firearm murders in a place-and-means sample of 5,396 counts: 2,552 (Western Cape 732, Gauteng 675, KwaZulu-Natal 560). Official PDF, slide 28.

Where the official murder rate is highest

Proven Eastern Cape has the highest official provincial murder ratio, 16.2 per 100,000, then Western Cape 14.4. Limpopo has the lowest, 2.6. Gauteng has the most murders by count (1,418) but a below-average ratio (8.1). The full table and a province-level map are on the heat map page. No map on this site uses a location finer than a police station, and the choropleth itself stops at province.

Proven Official murder top stations, July–September 2026 (PDF slide 22), first ten: Khayelitsha 66, Delft 66, Harare 65, Kraaifontein 57, Inanda 53, Mfuleni 53, Nyanga 50, Lwandle 48, Umlazi 48, Kwazakele 45. Twelve of the official top 30 stations are in the City of Cape Town District. This is a station-level ranking, not a map of private addresses.

Sources: SAPS Q2 2026/27 official PDF; SAPS annual-report briefing, 22 October 2026; written reply NW2957.
