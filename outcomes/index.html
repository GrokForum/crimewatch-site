Case outcomes, from docket to conviction

Back to CrimeWatch SA

This page is an attrition view from recorded crime to detection, court and conviction. A detection rate is not the same thing as “solved”, and a conviction rate is not a share of all reported cases. SAPS detection is the department’s performance measure on reported charges. SAPS-reported conviction rates are the share of cases that reach and finish in court. The 2025/26 briefing itself notes that conviction sits with the NPA, with SAPS in a supporting investigative role. The NPA, in a ministerial reply, also says it does not measure a “prosecution rate”, only conviction rates. The two conviction series are shown side by side and are not averaged. “Charges incomplete” in the SAPS table includes work carried over from earlier years, so it can be larger than a single year’s new reports. Detection and conviction rates are not multiplied together here, because they have different bases.

Official SAPS and NPA statistics record what was charged or counted, not what happened in every case in the country.

Historical attrition in a Gauteng rape-docket sample

Case data from its stated period, not mixed into the 2025/26 table. Established as what that study reported about its sample. Tracking Justice (published 2008) followed 2,064 rape dockets from Gauteng in 2003: 50.5% resulted in an arrest, 42.8% of those arrested were charged in court, trials started in fewer than one in five cases, and 6.2% of the original dockets ended in a conviction.
